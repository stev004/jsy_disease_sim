## 1. Estimand

I recommend **22 independent candidate libraries at R = 12, each scored against the same 20 fresh targets**. The primary estimand is \(p_{12}(\mathcal T)\), the probability that a fresh library passes all ten arm-(i) predicates on the realized target panel \(\mathcal T\). This is an operational measure of four-dimensional recovery at the frozen truth, grid, scenario and objective. The library is the unit of replication.

I propose **80% library reliability** as the decision standard. That is a judgment for the owner to rule on. It allows up to one failed library in five in the underlying draw distribution and has a different meaning from the 80% per-dimension target threshold.

- **Yes** means the study supports \(p_{12}(\mathcal T)>0.80\).
- **No** means the study supports \(p_{12}(\mathcal T)<0.80\). Mixed verdicts show that the draw can flip the result on identical targets. Uniform failure shows that recovery was absent across the draws tested.
- **Can’t tell** means the evidence leaves the 80% standard unresolved, or a shared target predicate prevents the library comparison from answering the question. Mixed verdicts can demonstrate a draw effect even when the reliability standard remains unresolved.

The conditioning on the target panel belongs in every reading. This is synthetic evidence for a later Steven decision. Real-data fitting remains STOPPED. [G41 owner ruling, §§3–5](/home/steven/jos-g42-sol-ro/docs/research/v1_3/2026-11-20-g41-owner-ruling.md:38)

## 2. Number of libraries and targets

**\(L=22\).** Choose the count by requiring the design to support a positive reading with one observed failure and a one-sided tail of at most 5% at the proposed reliability boundary. Under the independent-library draw model, \(P_{0.8}(K\ge L-1)\) is 5.76% at 21 libraries and 4.80% at 22. So 22 is the smallest count that meets both requirements. Allowing one failure avoids making the positive reading hinge on unanimity. The 80% standard and the 5% evidence threshold are proposed design choices for the owner. Neither comes from the retained outcomes or bootstrap.

This is a demanding confirmation design. For illustration, if conditional reliability were 90%, the positive reading would occur about 34% of the time. At 95%, it would occur about 70% of the time. Those are operating characteristics under hypothetical probabilities. Each extra library buys another draw and costs 300 latent calls and 7,500 report transforms.

**\(N=20\).** Doubling from ten makes each target worth five percentage points in the hit and boundary counts. It reduces the influence of a single unusual target and gives more opportunity to see whether the same targets drive disagreement across libraries. Ten more targets cost ten builds, ten latent calls and ten transforms for the whole campaign, plus scoring against every library and reference batch. Larger panels would further stabilize the fractional counts while giving the all-target viability and no-tie predicates more chances to bind. Their joint operating behavior is unknown. The owner should rule on \(N\), and Steven should receive the fraction-preserving change and its operating-characteristic table.

## 6. What I would change in the framing

I would call this **conditional gate reliability**, and report draw sensitivity alongside it. “Recovery is a property of R = 12” can sound like invariance or a claim about arbitrary fresh targets. A design that must generalize over both axes needs multiple independently drawn target panels crossed with independently drawn libraries, with a separate declaration of how to read the two sources of variation.

I would also distinguish a more stable fit from an explanation of the ridge. At a fixed cell and date, the expected squared distance from a fixed transformed target to a candidate mean includes a candidate-variance term divided by \(R\). If that variance differs across cells, changing \(R\) can change the expected finite-library loss ordering as well as its scatter. The exploratory ridge diagnostic supports examining both fixed-cell losses and re-profiled offsets. It leaves a joint beta–offset–detection tradeoff plausible. [Ridge diagnostic, §§Q3–Q5](/home/steven/jos-g42-sol-ro/docs/research/v1_3/2026-09-29-phase0b-ridge-diagnostic-EXPLORATORY.md:41)

Use a fixed design and complete every blind score before any truth join. If Steven’s decision requires target-panel generalization or finer discrimination near 80%, settle that before implementation. ## 8. Biggest risk and guard

The largest risk is that **one difficult target panel produces a precise answer to a narrow question**. A shared viability failure can force every verdict, and a few difficult targets can place many libraries at the same threshold. G35-B arm (i) met four inclusive counts exactly and had zero four-batch offset agreement. A verdict count alone would hide whether libraries disagree on many targets or flip because of one or two. ## Where I expect other designers to differ

Some will prefer fewer libraries and a descriptive count. That can show a same-panel flip at lower cost. My proposal spends more to put an explicit evidence standard behind a positive reliability claim. Others may choose unanimity, a different reliability threshold, or multiple target panels. Those choices reflect different tolerances for library failures or a different population of targets, and should be settled by the owner before sizing the study.
