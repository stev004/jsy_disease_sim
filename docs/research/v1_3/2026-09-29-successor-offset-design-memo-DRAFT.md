## Option 1 — Fix timing from external evidence

**Assumption and information.** A dated, independently justified event or sufficiently narrow interval locates the modeled inoculation relative to a declared simulation start. This needs an event-date definition, provenance, and a defensible link between the real event and JOS’s scheduled import acquisition. A first report date, an inbound screening count, or passenger volume alone does not establish infection date. The inspected JOS controls do not currently provide such a fixed offset. The synthetic truth `2` cannot be carried over as one.

**Claim.** Beta and detection could be estimated *conditional on the externally fixed timing and seeding representation*. JOS could not claim to have recovered or estimated timing; uncertainty or error in the external date would need a declared sensitivity analysis. If the external evidence only supplies a range, this becomes Option 3 or a set of conditional fits.

**Checks before any real fit.** A deterministic fixture must prove that the cited event date maps to the intended schedule, that attempts, acquisitions and reports retain their distinct dates, and that an unsupported date fails closed. A newly frozen blind synthetic-recovery gate must recover beta and both detection probabilities with timing fixed independently of target reports, and must test a deliberately wrong fixed date. Its wrong-delay control needs a new predicate: Phase 0/0b’s “selected offset equals 4” clause cannot apply when offset is fixed.

**Rough cost.** One offset gives **274 cells, 47 latent calls and 827 transforms** under the cost convention. That is materially below Phase 0b, plausibly a few minutes at the measured CI scale; 578 seconds cannot be rescaled into a measured runtime for this design.

## Option 2 — Profile timing as a nuisance

**Assumption and information.** Timing is uncertain but can be represented by a finite, defensible set of seeding schedules. For each schedule, fit beta and detection; publish their conditional results and the complete timing profile. If the profile is flat, multimodal, boundary-truncated, or gives materially different beta or detection results, report that limitation rather than selecting a convenient offset. Real application still needs a sourced definition of the plausible schedules relative to the chosen era and observation regime. JOS does not yet have that definition. Phase 0b’s synthetic `0–4` days is not a Jersey bound.

The existing loss supports **profiling by minimization**. Treating `exp(−loss)` as a probability, averaging “posterior” offsets, or calling the result marginalization would be unjustified. Probabilistic marginalization would require a declared observation likelihood and timing distribution, both separately checked.

**Claim.** A successful successor could claim synthetic recovery of beta and detection **conditional on each timing schedule**, or robustness across a predeclared admissible set. It could claim timing is unresolved where that is what the profile shows. It could not claim a recovered inoculation date, nor a single unconditional beta or detection estimate when their answers vary substantially with timing.

**Checks before any real fit.** A deterministic fixture must independently calculate a small complete loss surface, its per-offset conditional minima and its profiled minima; test ties, missing cells, boundary flags and read-back without truth-informed selection. A newly frozen recovery gate must place synthetic truths at multiple admissible timing values, blind the fitter to them, and require predeclared beta/detection recovery and stability criteria across timing. It must include a known confounded or weak-information case that is labelled unresolved rather than precise. Wrong-delay, wrong-ascertainment and negative-control rules need fresh declarations suited to conditional outputs.

**Rough cost.** Profiling the same five offset candidates retains approximately **1,334 cells, 107 latent calls and 4,007 transforms** across analogous arms: about the measured **578-second** Phase-0b scale before any new fixture or analysis overhead. Computing the profile from an already scored surface is cheap; generating that surface is the cost.

## Option 3 — Constrain timing with a sourced bound or prior

**Assumption and information.** External evidence rules out some schedules or gives defensible relative support to them. It needs a provenance-bearing event chronology and an account of infection-to-detection/report timing under the selected era. JOS’s annual arrivals control and demonstration delays supply neither a timing bound nor prior weights for a real infection event. A bound based only on the observed report curve would use the fitting target twice unless that dependence were explicitly modeled.

A **hard bound** restricts candidate schedules. A **prior** weights them; it does not add timing information merely by being numeric. Under the current minimum-distance loss, prior-weighted probabilistic claims require a separate likelihood-based design. A penalty added to the loss could be declared, but its weights and interpretation would need independent justification.

**Claim.** Results would be conditional on the bound or prior and its source. Tight constraints might make beta and detection recoverable while leaving timing identified mainly by the external assumption. The report would have to show sensitivity to defensible alternative bounds or weights and identify any truth excluded by the bound.

**Checks before any real fit.** A deterministic fixture must verify allowed-schedule membership, weight normalization if used, exact handling of zero support, and source-to-config provenance. The new recovery gate must test truths throughout the declared support and at its edges, plus an excluded-truth case that fails or reports misspecification. It must check beta/detection recovery and expose when a prior or boundary, rather than reports, determines timing.

**Rough cost.** If `K` offset candidates remain and the other illustrative grids and arms stay as above, the analogous total is **`265K + 9` cells, `32 + 15K` latent calls, and `800K + 27` transforms**. Thus `K=1` has Option 1’s 274/47/827 counts; `K=5` has Phase 0b’s 1,334/107/4,007 counts. This is a cost formula, **not a suggested value of `K`**. Runtime spans roughly the corresponding few-minute to Phase-0b scale; likelihood development or changed source processing is unmeasured.

## Option 4 — Change the information or the seeding model

**Assumption and information.** The present observation design, time horizon, or one-day pulse may be the reason timing cannot be separated from other effects. This is a **hypothesis**, not a diagnosis from Phase 0/0b. Possible designs are a longer observation window; an additional, semantically verified report or testing series; or a seeding model such as initial prevalence, repeated imports, or dated import pressure. The V1.3 plan already anticipates a later cases/tests/positivity/serology objective and broad import pressure if an era needs it. Such data can constrain different aspects of the epidemic, but their ability to identify inoculation timing is unproven.

JOS can configure initial seed count, initial prevalence, scheduled imports and a daily import rate. The imported-versus-local simulator attribution is available internally. The frozen daily surveillance and serosurvey may support a different observation model after event-date, regime, suppression and denominator review. Annual 2025 passenger arrivals cannot be treated as a dated 2020 infected-arrival series; arrival infection prevalence and infection age are unresolved inputs. Changing from a pulse to another seeding representation changes the scientific question and may require protected lifecycle work. [Run controls](/home/steven/jos-g34b-readonly/src/jersey_outbreak/outbreak_schemas.py); [V1.3 plan](/home/steven/jos-g34b-readonly/docs/research/v1_3/2026-09-21-v13-plan.md); [travel design evidence](/home/steven/jos-g34b-readonly/docs/research/v1_1/R4_TRAVEL_BEHAVIOUR_UNCERTAINTY.md).

**Claim.** A successor could claim timing recovery only for the new, explicitly defined seeding parameter and observation model after its own gate passes. Recovery of a pulse offset would not establish recovery of an import-pressure history or infection ages. Adding a series could improve identification, but cannot be assumed to do so.

**Checks before any real fit.** A deterministic fixture must reconcile seeded, imported and local acquisitions, report chronology, horizon tails, missing and suppressed observations, and every new series’ date semantics. A controlled synthetic study should hold seeds, population builds, truth and scoring choices fixed while changing **only** window length or added series; separately compare seeding representations using explicitly matched estimands. The fresh blind recovery gate must test the chosen final design across process seeds, with negative controls and misspecification cases that expose false timing precision.

**Rough cost.** A longer window with the same grid leaves **1,334 cells and 107 latent calls**, but each latent call covers more than 30 days and each transform covers more dates; wall time is **above or below no defensible numeric estimate from the 578-second measurement** without a pilot. An added series can leave cell and latent-call counts unchanged while increasing transform and scoring work. A different seeding grid changes latent-call counts and needs a new dry-run calculation.

## Option 5 — Drop timing from the fitted set and state the loss of scope

**Assumption and information.** The project accepts a declared seeding *scenario* without claiming its timing is known or inferred. This is possible with current JOS controls and does not require inventing an external date, but a scenario date remains an assumption. It differs from Option 1’s evidence-fixed timing and Option 2’s explicit accounting for timing uncertainty.

**Claim.** The fitted set would contain beta and the two detection probabilities only, all **conditional on the stated seeding scenario**. The project would lose the planned claim that it recovers a seeding quantity, and a result could change under another plausible timing. Dropping the seeding dimension from V1.3’s planned target would require Steven to revise that scope. A conditional synthetic pass could not be described as a pass of either four-dimensional Phase 0 gate.

**Checks before any real fit.** A deterministic fixture must show that timing is a recorded scenario input, never optimized from target reports, and that outputs do not contain a timing estimate or precision field. A fresh blind recovery gate must recover the three remaining quantities under several separately fixed timing scenarios and predeclare how much cross-scenario variation is acceptable. If results are unstable, the gate must fail or narrow its claims accordingly.

**Rough cost.** One declared scenario has Option 1’s illustrative **274 cells, 47 latent calls and 827 transforms**, plausibly a few minutes at CI scale. Repeating over `K` timing scenarios approaches Option 3’s `K`-dependent cost; it is not one cheap fit followed by an uncosted sensitivity claim.

## Recommendation

**Develop Option 2—profile inoculation timing as a nuisance—as the next *design candidate*.** It uses JOS’s existing complete-grid machinery, acknowledges that the current evidence does not fix a real infection date, and keeps the seeding uncertainty visible while asking whether beta and both detection probabilities are stable across plausible schedules. Its main risk is decisive: the profiles may show that those three quantities also change materially with timing. If so, the successor should report non-identification and remain stopped; profiling is a way to expose that result, not a route around the failed gate.

Before a predeclaration, the model owner would need to define what timing means in the selected real-data era and identify a source-supported admissible set. If no such set can be defended, Option 5 is the honest narrower scope for Steven to consider. If a candidate later changes the observation objective or seeding representation, Option 4’s separate fixture and recovery work becomes necessary.

## What Steven would need to decide next

1. Whether a successor should pursue conditional recovery with timing profiled, narrow V1.3 to scenario-conditional three-parameter work, or remain stopped.
2. Whether the available frozen evidence can justify a real-era seeding schedule or bound; if not, whether to seek additional dated infection/import evidence or change the seeding estimand.
3. Whether to authorize a **separate** owner-ruled design and frozen predeclaration, followed by implementation review and a new synthetic-recovery gate. This memo grants none of those permissions.
