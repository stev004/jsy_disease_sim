# JOS independent audit — ranked findings

**Bundle:** `jos-claude-science-audit-2026-09-03`, `main` @ `df41196` · **Date:** 2026-09-03
**Mandate:** the next round of optimization and improvement beyond R6 and the R7 chain, plus anything wrong, fragile or scientifically questionable.
**Reviewers:** four independent lanes (route kernel; disease/observation/ensemble; provenance/artifacts/API; data/population/travel/calibration) plus cross-cutting re-derivation of the measured evidence. 51 findings.

---

## Verdict

The R7 chain worked. On the project's own two samples the marginal simulated-day cost fell 15.58 → 2.26 s/day and the 30-day wall fell 462.9 → 114.0 s, with M4, latent, outcome and observation hashes unchanged. Nothing in this audit disputes that.

What this audit disputes is **what should happen next**, on three grounds.

**First, the Amdahl model that ranked the remaining work is now inverted.** Re-derived from the bundled measurements, a 44-replicate ensemble at 7 workers spends **32.6% of its wall time outside the marginal simulated day** — 15.1% serial parent build, 9.8% per-worker M4 regeneration, 7.7% per-replicate fixed initialization. Pre-R7 those same three terms were about 8%. R7 ranked them third, fourth and fifth and dismissed `Sim.init()` at "1.3% of a 48-minute replicate"; at 452 s per replicate that fixed component is **10.2%**. The kernel now has a ceiling of 67% of ensemble wall, and each further 10% off the marginal day buys 6.7% of the ensemble.

**Second, the numbers everything rests on were measured on a configuration that is not the production configuration.** The 2.26 s/day slope comes from two wall-clock samples (7 d, 30 d) of one seed, in the epidemic's steepest phase, **with no intervention manager attached and no travel layer** — while the 44-replicate ensembles exist to run M7 scenarios over 180 days. R6's own benchmark protocol demanded 7/14/30-day runs with interleaved repeats and a median-plus-range; the chain gate recorded two points and no repeats. Two independent measurements point the same way and in opposite directions: the intervention layer adds a measured ~0.5 s/day that no profile has ever seen (DISEASE-1), and the landed attribution lookup crosses a numpy size threshold at ~58 successes per direction where its cost steps up **8.9×** (DISEASE-7) — a threshold a 180-day run spends most of its time above. The 7.5-minute replicate should be treated as a lower bound for a baseline scenario and as unmeasured for an M7 scenario.

**Third, two of the gates that certify this work do not test what they are said to test.** The attribution reference oracle compares two implementations defined in the test file, neither of which is the shipped algorithm — none of the int64 packing, the `np.isin` filter or the per-pair deque introduced by Stage-2 is under test (DISEASE-2). The route fingerprint harness covers 7 of 11 routes, never touches the Starsim `p1/p2/beta/dur` boundary the Network gate explicitly requires, cannot detect a route that vanished from both sides of a comparison, and measures wall time through a Python wrapper around the hottest function in the system, biasing the reported factor upward on its hash-attributable component (ROUTE-2). And the largest planned speedup — 4 → 7 workers — **cannot pass its own equivalence gate**, because `workers`, `estimated_worker_memory_bytes` and `memory_safety_fraction` are hashed into the M6 ensemble logical identity and the artifact_id (PROV-1).

Beyond that, one published scientific output is wrong: ensemble incidence bands report a quantile over **fabricated zeros** for dates outside each metric's own horizon — 120 of 9,752 summary rows in a 180-day 44-replicate ensemble report median = lower = upper = 0.0 labelled `stochastic_replicate_quantile` with 44 contributing replicates, at the right-hand edge of every band plot (DISEASE-4). Independently, the immutable P4 comparator states a horizon of "180 dated points (2025-01-06 → 2025-07-08)" when 2025-07-08 is point **184** — the four extra days are exactly the observation tail, i.e. the same dates the fabricated zeros occupy (CROSS-3).

The provenance and evidence machinery is the strongest part of the codebase and largely survived adversarial reading: the write-once `SUCCEEDED` anchor has no bypass, the verifiers recompute identities from content and trust a stored checksum nowhere, the tamper tests are genuinely adversarial, and the M8 episode-time attribution contract holds up. Blockers B01, B02, B03, M01 and M04 from the 2026-09-01 audit are closed (B01 with one missed writer). B04 and M03 are **not assessable from this bundle** — it contains no CI definition and no release-instruction document.

## Method and limits

Static review of all 138 bundled files, ~50k lines. **No JOS pipeline stage was executed:** the bundle omits `data/raw` and `data/processed` snapshots and Starsim 3.5.2 is not installed. Consequences that travel with every number below:

- Every wall-time figure attributed to this audit is either arithmetic over the bundled evidence artifacts, or a **sandbox micro-benchmark of a function extracted verbatim from the source**, driven by synthetic inputs sized from the committed baseline JSON, on Apple-silicon hardware — **not** the Ryzen 7 5800X / 26 GB WSL2 host the ground-truth numbers come from. These compare two implementations of the same pure function; they are not production timings and are labelled where used.
- Per-event totals for a 180-day run are projections: **no 180-day event count exists anywhere in the bundle.** The pilot's 294,565 episodes came from a waning-enabled configuration (see CROSS-1), while the current default disables waning and therefore caps episodes near the 104,540 population.
- `pyarrow` was not installed, so Parquet write cost is unmeasured. `staffing_generator.py` internals, `intervention_analysis.py` and parts of `interventions.py` were read selectively rather than in full; `travel_artifacts.py`, `population_schemas.py` and `population_structure_schemas.py` were checked by targeted grep against specific claims. Every `file:line` cited was confirmed by a direct read of that range.
- B04, M03 and the mypy module list are unverifiable as bundled (CROSS-4).

### CROSS-1 · The performance target's evidence base is not the production configuration
**Category** reproducibility · **Risk** sci low / repro **high** · **Effort** low · **Confidence** high
**Path** `docs/runs/2026-09-03-r7-chain-hash-gate.json`; `docs/runs/2026-09-02-r6-mem-profile-desktop.json`; `configs/diseases/respiratory_seirs_demo.yaml`; R6 profile "Benchmark protocol" step 2.

R6's 66% route / 34% respiratory split — the prioritization model for the whole programme — was therefore derived on a workload with roughly 3.6× the event load the current default produces, biasing it toward attribution.

**Change** Publish a three-parameter cost model (fixed init + per-simulated-day route cost + per-event cost) fitted on 7/14/30/60-day runs with per-phase timers, and re-state which disease configuration each historical number was produced under.
**Confirm or kill** Run 7/14/30/60-day replicates with per-phase timers plus one real 180-day replicate; report the days 120–180 marginal slope separately from days 8–30. Kill the concern if the two slopes agree within 15%.
**Gates** randomness+identity (re-derive one committed hash under each named config to settle which the pilot used); memory at 7/14/30/60/120/180 days.

### PROV-1 · The M6 ensemble logical hash contains worker count — the planned 4→7 worker change cannot pass its own gate
**Category** reproducibility · **Risk** sci low / repro medium · **Effort** medium · **Confidence** high
**Path** `ensemble_schemas.py:28-33`; `scientific_hashes.py:139-181`; `scientific_verification.py:419`; `job_finalizer.py`.

`m6_ensemble_logical_hash` serialises the whole `EnsembleConfig`, which carries four pure execution-resource fields: `workers`, `estimated_worker_memory_bytes`, `memory_safety_fraction`, `allow_unsafe_workers`. That hash sets `base_config_hash` and the last 12 hex of `artifact_id`, and the verifier asserts the binding. The same function deliberately strips per-replicate `runtime_seconds` — so the authors already accepted that execution telemetry must not enter the identity; `workers` was missed. Rerunning an identical ensemble at 7 workers instead of 4 produces a different logical hash and a different artifact_id for a reason with nothing to do with science.

**Change** Exclude the four execution fields from the hash payload and from `base_config_hash`; bump `M6_ENSEMBLE_ARTIFACT_SCHEMA_VERSION` 1.4 → 1.5 and branch the verifier on `manifest_schema_version` as it already does for legacy paths; drop `request.workers` from the finalizer's reconstructed comparison. No provenance is lost — requested/planned/actual workers are already recorded in the manifest and diagnostics.
**Confirm or kill** Run the small-mode ensemble fixture at `workers=1` and `workers=2`; compare logical hash and all three tables. Identical rows with a differing hash confirms it. **Kill condition:** if any trajectory row differs between worker counts, the hash difference is honest and the real finding is worker-count-dependent science — far more serious.
**Gates** randomness+identity (per-replicate seeds, draws, namespaces unchanged); network (per-replicate M4 hashes); disease+attribution (per-replicate `latent_outcome_hash` at 1 vs N workers); **new unit test**: ensemble logical hash invariant under all four execution fields.

### DISEASE-2 · The attribution reference oracle does not exercise the production lookup
**Category** correctness · **Risk** sci low / repro low · **Effort** low · **Confidence** high
**Path** `tests/test_respiratory_attribution_lookup.py:12-45, 48-73, 137-170` vs `respiratory.py:344-375`.

The brief presents "reference-oracle tests committed" as the gate on the Stage-2 rewrite. The test file never imports `RespiratorySEIRS` and never calls `_order_invariant_infect`. It defines `_old_lookup` (the pre-Stage-2 `probability_by_pair[...].pop(0)` reference) and `_indexed_lookup`, and asserts those two agree. `_indexed_lookup` is **not** the shipped algorithm: production packs `(src, trg)` into int64 with `K = 2**32`, filters with `np.isin`, and builds a `defaultdict(deque)` over surviving hit indices consumed with `popleft()`. None of the packing, the injectivity assumption, the membership test or the deque is under test. Traced by hand, the equivalence **does** hold — but the gate would pass unchanged if the packing collided, if the assert were disabled, or if a future edit broke the lookup. Everything downstream of candidate construction — the per-target candidate sort (`:407-412`), the stable attribution draw (`:415-429`) and the entire evidence payload (`:430-441`), all named as protected contracts — has **no oracle at all**; its only coverage is a 3-agent, 2-route permutation test.

**Change** Extract the lookup block into a module-level pure function called unchanged from `_order_invariant_infect`, and re-point the existing fixtures and randomised test at it. Add fixtures the current file cannot express: UIDs near the packing bound; a pair whose earliest occurrence has a materially different beta from the successful one; a success list permuted relative to edge order. Extend the oracle past candidate construction to the sorted candidate list, the draw and the evidence dict.
**Confirm or kill** Mutation test, three mutants, each must turn the re-pointed oracle red: `K = 2**16`; `popleft()` → `pop()`; drop the `np.isin` filter and index `arange(len(src))`. **Confirm that the current test file stays green under all three** — that is the demonstration that the present gate is vacuous.
**Gates** disease+attribution (full-scale byte-identical latent outcome hash before/after extraction on frozen 7/14/30-day runs); randomness+identity (`trans_rng` call count and order); the eight named fixture classes plus a packing-bound case.

### ROUTE-2 · The route fingerprint harness does not gate what the Stage-1 acceptance list claims
**Category** reproducibility · **Risk** sci low / repro low · **Effort** low · **Confidence** high
**Path** `scripts/bench_dynamic_routes.py:120, 47-79, 143-149, 28-33`; `tests/test_bench_dynamic_routes.py:36-54`.

Four defects in the instrument that certifies every change in the hottest path. It fingerprints only `sorted(generated._dynamic_builders)` — **7 routes**; `household`, `school_class`, `care_resident` and `care_staff` are never fingerprinted at any date, although `school_class` has calendar `weekday_term` and is daily-replaced in Starsim. `_compare` builds its key set as `set(first) | set(second)`, so a route absent from **both** files produces no mismatch and the tool prints "fingerprints identical" — precisely the failure signature of an optimization that reclassifies a route as static or precomputed, which is the class of change this audit keeps proposing. It fingerprints edge dicts only, never the Starsim `p1/p2/beta/dur` arrays the Network gate explicitly requires, and never the M4-immutability clause. It clears `_snapshot_cache` before every date and iterates route-major, so the production date-major LRU pattern and its eviction path are never exercised. And it measures wall time through a monkeypatched wrapper around `_stable_int`, sandbox-measured at **+14.7% per call** — with 40,719,667 calls the committed 242.06 s baseline carries ~6.9 s of pure instrumentation, and any change that removes hash calls is credited at the instrumented rate, so the reported Stage-1b 5.11× is an upper bound on its hash-attributable component. Finally the 30-day gate window (2025-01-06 … 2025-02-04) contains **no school term boundary** — the first half-term is 2025-02-17.

R7 specified this gate as "all 11 routes for every one of the 180 exercised dates" plus "exact Starsim p1, p2, beta, dur dtype, shape, order and bytes". The shipped harness is 7 routes × 30 dates and no arrays.

**Change** Iterate `sorted(generated.route_specs)`, not `_dynamic_builders`; make `_compare` fail when the route set differs from a declared expected list recorded in the JSON meta; add a second per-(route, date) fingerprint over `_edge_arrays` output; add a date-major mode that does not clear the cache and records final `len(_snapshot_cache)`; move the call counter inside `_stable_int` behind a module flag so wall time is uninstrumented; extend the standard window across 2025-02-14 … 2025-02-25 and add a committed ci-mode fingerprint fixture so a semantic regression fails CI.
**Confirm or kill** Delete one route from `_dynamic_builders` in a scratch branch, run the harness twice and `--compare` — it must currently exit 0. Perturb `persistence_days` on `school_class` and confirm the harness still reports identical. Re-run the committed benchmark with the wrapper disabled and report the delta against 242.06 s.
**Gates** network (this finding *is* the instrument for it); randomness+identity (cross-process repeat of the fingerprint run).

## Tier 1 — published-output and provenance correctness

### DISEASE-4 · Ensemble incidence bands report a quantile of fabricated zeros outside the metric's own horizon
**Category** scientific · **Risk** sci low / repro **high** · **Effort** medium · **Confidence** high
**Path** `ensemble.py:437-440` vs `:442-444`; `:425-431`; `observation.py:131-145`; `tests/test_c4_contracts.py:326-327`; `travel.py:3108`.

`_completed_grid_rows` completes the replicate × date grid per metric semantic. For `state` metrics a date outside the replicate's observed range becomes `outside_metric_horizon`, value `None`, `contributes=False`. For `incidence` metrics **any** missing date becomes `0.0` with `cell_semantic='structural_zero'` and `contributes=True`, with no notion of the metric's own horizon. Because `_trajectory_rows` emits a dense row for every date each table actually covers, a genuinely quiet day inside the horizon always has a row — so the structural-zero branch fires **only** outside the horizon, which is exactly where it is wrong. The grid's date union is 4 days longer than the latent horizon (`observation_demo.yaml` has `analysis_horizon_tail_days: null`, so the derived tail = natural-history 2 + detection max 0 + reporting max 2 = 4), and every latent incidence metric is credited with a real zero on those days. Reproduced with the production functions: **120 of 9,752 summary rows** for a 44-replicate 180-day ensemble report median = lower = upper = 0.0 with `contributing_replicates = 44` and `interval_class = 'stochastic_replicate_quantile'`. A plotted median band drops to zero for the last four days of every ensemble figure. The same repository's M8 summariser declares the **opposite** rule (`outside_horizon_behavior = 'excluded'`), which is the evidence that this is an oversight, not a global choice.

**Change** Pass the per-replicate latent and observed horizons into `_completed_grid_rows` and, in the incidence branch, emit `structural_zero` only when `observations[0][0] <= when <= observations[-1][0]`, otherwise `outside_metric_horizon` — the identical test the state branch already uses. Reconcile with `travel.py:3101-3111` so one rule governs both summarisers. This changes the M6 ensemble logical hash by construction: it is a **versioned scientific correction**, not an exact-semantics optimization. Bump the schema version and record the prior hash.
**Confirm or kill** Two-replicate fixture whose observation horizons differ by 3 days; assert the shorter replicate contributes no cell beyond its own last observed date for any incidence metric. Then diff one frozen ensemble summary — only tail-date incidence rows may change. **Kill if any within-horizon row moves**: that would mean genuine quiet days were being suppressed.
**Gates** ensemble (every within-horizon row byte-identical; cumulative and state semantics untouched); reproducibility (prior hash recorded, schema bumped in the same commit); `test_c4_contracts.py:326-327` rewritten with an explicit within-horizon quiet-day case **and** a beyond-horizon case, so the two are distinguished rather than conflated.

### ROUTE-6 · `activity_cv` is mathematically inert on two of the four routes the M4 diagnostics declare it applies to
**Category** scientific · **Risk** sci low / repro low · **Effort** low · **Confidence** high
**Path** `network_generator.py:1133-1139`, `:1247-1253`, `:189-193`, `:235-236`, `:69-74`, `:1941-1943`; `tests/test_networks.py:340-353`.

For `school_cross_class` the requested `expected_participant_count` is the sum of all school-year group sizes and the eligible set is exactly the union of those groups; for `workplace_transient` it is the size of the distinct active set against the same eligible ids. In both cases `expected >= len(ordered)` is unconditionally true, so `_activity_participation_probabilities` returns 1.0 for every agent **at any `activity_cv`** and the whole eligible set is returned. Verified with the extracted functions at `activity_cv=0.5`: the returned set equals the input set for both call shapes, while the community call shape (expected = 0.6n) correctly selects 3000/5000. The M4 diagnostics nevertheless publish `approved_routes` = all four `CONTACT_ACTIVITY_ROUTES` with `application='participation_once'` and `sensitivity_required=True`. **Any `activity_cv` sensitivity result is a two-route sensitivity described as a four-route one.** No test catches it: `test_networks.py:340-353` asserts only that `school_cross_class` staff edges are unchanged under `activity_cv=0.5` — which is what an inert route looks like.

`activity_cv` defaults to 0.0 and diagnostics record `shipped_nonzero_default=False`, so no shipped baseline result is affected; the exposure is to sensitivity runs and to any future non-zero default.

**Change** Two separable actions. **Scientific:** decide whether activity weighting is intended for these routes. If yes, the expected count must be a fraction of the eligible pool — a model change requiring the normal scenario-assumption and provenance route, not a performance commit. If no, remove the two calls and narrow `CONTACT_ACTIVITY_ROUTES` and the diagnostics' `approved_routes` to the two community routes. Until decided, the diagnostics block must not claim a four-route application. **Performance (independent, exact):** the all-one path still does `sorted(set(...))`, builds an n-entry dict of 1.0, scans it with `all()`, builds a set, and the caller filters every group against it — four O(n) or O(n log n) passes for a provable no-op (sandbox: 14.6 ms at n=34,000 plus 4.3 ms at n=13,000 ≈ 19 ms/day).
**Confirm or kill** Build one ci-mode M4 at `activity_cv=0.0` and one at 0.5, all else fixed; assert `school_cross_class` and `workplace_transient` snapshots are equal for every snapshot date while `community_indoor` differs.
**Gates** network (every route × date edge sequence identical at both `activity_cv` values after removing the calls); randomness+identity (confirm no draw is removed from a stream that is actually consumed); disease+attribution on frozen runs.

### ROUTE-7 · Declared edge persistence is not implemented, and a dead pre-sort is what makes it look implemented
**Category** correctness / performance · **Risk** sci low / repro low · **Effort** low · **Confidence** high
**Path** `network_generator.py:1238-1246`, `:309-331`, `:1264`, `:1150`, `:360-365`, `:559-575`; `starsim_adapter.py:109`.

`build_workplace_transient` sorts each workplace's active members with a key containing the ISO week, then passes the groups to `_grouped_ring_edges`, which immediately **re-sorts every group** on a key containing the full ISO date. Since `_ordered_ids` sorts on `(_stable_int(...), agent_id)` — a total order that is a function of the member multiset only — the second sort completely determines the ring order and the first sort's output is discarded. Verified empirically: over 300 synthetic workplace groups, `_grouped_ring_edges` returns byte-identical edge lists whether groups arrive pre-sorted, unsorted, shuffled, or with a duplicated member. So the first sort costs a full pass of SHA-256 draws plus a sort for nothing — and its ISO-week token was the route's **only** expression of a weekly refresh period, so `workplace_transient` in fact re-randomises its ring every single day while declaring `persistence 'periodically_refreshed'` with `persistence_days=7`. The identical question applies to `school_cross_class`, which declares 14 days and also re-draws staff start offsets from the date.

## Recommended implementation order

**Stage A — restore the gates (before any optimization).** PROV-1 (excise execution fields from the ensemble identity, schema 1.4→1.5) unblocks the worker change; DISEASE-2 (re-point the attribution oracle at the shipped lookup, prove it with three mutants) makes the highest-risk landed change testable; ROUTE-2 (11 routes, Starsim arrays, declared route list, uninstrumented timing, a term-boundary window, a committed ci fixture) makes the route gate mean what the acceptance list claims; ROUTE-10 (one `_stable_int` with a pinned digest) is a prerequisite for ROUTE-1 and is the cheapest reproducibility insurance in the codebase. All four are low-effort and none needs a full-scale run. **Nothing in Stage B should merge before these are green.**

**Stage B — re-measure, then re-rank.** CROSS-1 and CROSS-2 are one measurement campaign: 7/14/30/60-day replicates plus one real 180-day replicate, with per-phase timers (parent build decomposed into M2/M3/M4, per-worker M4 regeneration, `Sim.init()`, route generation, attribution, post-processing, canonical hashing, Parquet write, observation), and — the single most conspicuous gap in the entire performance record — **one 30-day replicate under `m7_community_indoor` and one under `m7_combined`** (DISEASE-1's confirm step). Add the memory campaign from DISEASE-11 to the same run. Expected outcome: a three-parameter cost model, a settled overhead decomposition, and either confirmation or refutation of the 7.5-minute projection.

**Stage C — the overhead block, which Stage B should show is now the largest single target.** ROUTE-11 (diagnostics switch — provably hash-neutral, since diagnostics are outside the M4 logical hash) and the incremental M4 hash; DATA-2 and DATA-1 (the M2 and M3 generator fixes, **gated on DATA-6's golden hashes, which must land first**); then verified M2/M3 parent reuse and the pool initializer from R7's lead 4, now worth 15% of ensemble wall rather than the ~5% the pre-R7 arithmetic implied. DISEASE-3 belongs here too: its 5 s is minor but its 0.6–1.1 GB of parent memory is exactly the budget the worker bound fails to model.

**Stage D — the kernel remainder, in measured order.** DISEASE-1 steps (1) and (2) (memoise the run-constant adherence predicate — the largest measured single factor in the audit at 10.2×, and the only one that touches the production M7 path); ROUTE-1 (prefix-hoisted digests, ~271 ms/day); ROUTE-3 (ordered merge, ~157 ms/day); ROUTE-7's two-line deletion (~41 ms/day); ROUTE-9 (community preamble, ~73 ms/day); DISEASE-7 (the `np.isin` size dispatch — small on speed, but it removes a step-change the 180-day run will sit above). ROUTE-5 (columnar edges) is the largest structural item and should be staged behind its own three-phase gate; ROUTE-4's weekday memo should follow it, not precede it, because in dict form the retained snapshots cost ~35 MB.

**Stage E — robustness, in parallel and independent of the above.** DISEASE-6 (per-replicate persistence) and DISEASE-5 (honest worker bound, corrected to 4–5) belong together: persistence is what makes a conservative bound cheap and an aggressive one survivable. PROV-2, PROV-9 and PROV-7 close the job-layer liveness, publication-ordering and submit-ordering holes. PROV-8 (one parent build, one scenario normalisation) is a prerequisite for parent reuse and should land before Stage C's reuse work.

**Scientific corrections, on their own release track with explicit hash migrations, not through an equivalence gate:** DISEASE-4 (incidence horizon), CROSS-3 (comparator horizon erratum), ROUTE-6 (activity_cv route scope), ROUTE-7's persistence question, DATA-10 (resident detections), DATA-7 (arrival-total provenance), DATA-8/DATA-9 (calibration claims), DISEASE-10 (agreement diagnostic and declared RNG key). Each changes a published output or a published claim; each needs a version bump and a recorded prior hash rather than a byte-identity gate.

## Would not do

**Hashing and draw identity.** Do not replace `_stable_int`'s SHA-256 with a PRNG, Python `hash()`, a vectorised hash, a different delimiter or a different encoding — every draw identity in M4, staffing, interventions and travel is defined by the exact UTF-8 byte string. Do not merge the two community draws into one digest to halve the hash count: two draws from one digest changes both values. Do not switch `canonical_json_bytes` to orjson/ujson/msgspec: third-party encoders differ on float repr, integer overflow, NaN/Infinity, non-string keys and unicode escaping, and any one silently invalidates every published hash including the frozen V1 and P4 evidence. Streaming `iterencode` with the identical encoder is the only safe throughput change, and it was verified byte-identical.

**Caching.** Do not enlarge the R6 snapshot LRU to buy day-loop hits — community, school_cross_class and workplace_transient snapshots are exact-date keyed by construction, so extra capacity buys retention without reuse. Do not add a `(weekday, ISO week)` memo for the **bus** route even though R7 groups it with workplace_team and shared_vehicle: its key cardinality is ~one state per simulated weekday. Do not memoize workplace_transient, school_cross_class or the community routes on any period shorter than the date. Do not persist all 180 days of snapshots or a per-seed full-horizon M4 — R7's ~2 GB/seed and ~88 GB/44-seed arithmetic still holds, and the measured 236 B per edge dict confirms the order of magnitude. Do not turn the M8 evidence stores into LRUs by analogy with R6: they are read after the run and feed `temporary_network_hash` and event identity, so eviction would corrupt the output or force recomputation with end-of-run state under a mid-run date.

**Streaming the M4 hash with `iterencode`.** Measured: on a 240,000-edge payload the streamed form produces the identical digest but takes **1.49 s against 0.27 s** — 5.5× slower. If the transient peak needs bounding, hand-assemble the container syntax and feed `json.dumps` of each sub-payload to a single sha256.

**Attribution.** Do not replace `np.isin` with `searchsorted` unconditionally: measured **4–6× slower** below numpy's size threshold, which is where every early-epidemic day sits. Only correct as an explicit size dispatch. Do not replace attribution with first-success, route counts, aggregate hazards or a new draw, and do not recompute transmission occurrence independently and treat a statistically equivalent mask as authoritative.

**Observation.** Do not remove the offline rebuild outright — the comparison **is** the online/offline equivalence check, and the measured cost is minutes of ensemble wall, not the run. Make it opt-out and fix the diagnostic. Do not pool or reuse observation RNG generators: every draw identity depends on the per-event seed. Do not optimise `_delay` or `rng.choice` in the scheduler — both bundled delay distributions are `kind='fixed'`, so that path never executes.

**Concurrency.** Do not raise workers to 7 on the strength of the existing footprint data: both the 3.36 GB observed RSS and the 3.5 GB constant come from 30-day runs, worker OOM has already killed this pipeline twice, and a broken pool currently discards every completed replicate. Measure a 180-day worker first and add persistence before spending headroom. Do not use 16 logical workers on the 8-core host — and note that nothing in the code currently prevents it. Do not parallelise route generation across threads within a replicate (GIL-bound dict/string/SHA-256 work contending with the pool), and do not parallelise M2 or M3 internally — both consume a single seeded Generator in strict sequential order, so concurrency destroys the population hash. The way to spend concurrency on parent construction is to build it **once** and reuse it.

**Generators and evidence.** Do not vectorise the age/sex pool draws as a *first* move: each ends in `rng.choice`/`rng.integers` over a candidate list whose order is the insertion order of a dict, so restructuring risks silently reordering candidates while still passing a determinism test that only compares two in-process runs — do DATA-6 first. Do not replace Hamilton/largest-remainder allocation: exactly specified, tie-broken by insertion order, RNG-independent, not a hot spot. Do not convert the generators' dict rows to numpy structured arrays or DataFrames — the logical hashes are computed over their canonical JSON and dtype coercion (None vs NaN, int64 vs int) lands exactly where the hash is taken. Do not drop the per-row pydantic validation to save ~1–3 s across ~330,000 rows: it catches precisely the class of error an optimization here would introduce. Do not weaken `_validate_canonical_inputs` or the source SHA-256 validation — hashing fourteen small CSVs is milliseconds and it is the fail-closed boundary of the whole evidence chain.

**Job layer and micro-optimizations.** Do not make the finalizer trust the worker's candidate hashes to avoid re-verifying: only the **adapter-side** pass is redundant; the finalizer's independent recomputation is the entire basis of the write-once success anchor. Do not add covering indexes to the job registry — `jobs_state_idx` already covers the FIFO scan and the measured cost is connection and transaction overhead on a table that will hold hundreds of rows. Do not backfill engine identity anchors on v1-migrated rows: the divergence is deliberate and backfilling would manufacture provenance that was never observed. Do not relax the M9 request bounds — the gap runs the other way: `replicate_seeds` has **no length cap**, so one request can enqueue an arbitrary number of full simulations. Do not optimise the provider's `list()` copy (1.5 ms per 150k edges) or `_load_starsim` (warm `sys.modules`). Do not raise `stream_scale` above 0.001 in the shipped configs before DATA-3 and DATA-4 land — the first thing a scale increase would produce is an apparently hung job.

## What holds up

Stated deliberately, because the brief invited adversarial reading and several things survived it.

The **finalizer and registry authority model** could not be broken. `LEGAL_TRANSITIONS` omits `SUCCEEDED` from every source set; `update_fields` has a four-column allowlist; `finalize_success` demands RUNNING plus complete matching identity anchors plus at least one artifact plus `verification_status=passed` under a `WHERE state='RUNNING'` guard, is idempotent for an identical repeat, and re-reads and re-hashes its own manifest before committing. No path to SUCCEEDED bypasses it. The verifiers **recompute identities from content everywhere examined and trust a stored checksum nowhere**, and the tamper tests are genuinely adversarial — they update a stored raw checksum and still expect failure, and they reject coordinated provenance substitution.

The **M8 episode-time attribution contract** is well defended and the three M8.1 blockers appear properly closed: slot reuse preserves event-time identity and resets state, a departed traveller's test result is historical and cannot touch the reused slot, results arriving on the final active timestep and returning-resident delayed results are covered, and all four event-time transmission directions are asserted. On reading, attribution does map through the active episode at the event timestep rather than through a final slot occupant.

The **evidence layer** is the strongest part of the data lane: every raw snapshot is validated against `data/sources.yaml` SHA-256 before parsing, `_validate_canonical_inputs` re-hashes every canonical table and refuses to proceed unless `quality_report.build_status == 'passed'`, observed rows carry per-row provenance with an explicit observation status, and the controls loaders hard-assert their reconciliation constants.

The **Stage-1b caches are correctly keyed.** The workday cache key covers seed, job_id, agent_id, days_per_week and remote_days_per_week — the complete dependency set — and is bounded by job-row count, not horizon. And the **Stage-2 attribution algorithm is, on reading, genuinely equivalent** to the old `pop(0)` semantics, including duplicate pairs where an earlier duplicate fails: `np.isin` marks every occurrence of a successful pair and the deque consumes them in ascending index order. DISEASE-2 is a criticism of the *gate*, not of the design.

On the ensemble side, **result ordering under process-pool completion, failed-replicate accounting and the insufficient-tail guard on quantiles are all correct**, and the bands are correctly named as stochastic replicate quantiles throughout — each was checked specifically. Blockers B01 (one writer excepted), B02, B03, M01 and M04 are closed, and B02 in particular is closed well: the daily table now carries `cohort_detected_infections`, a `cohort_ascertainment_fraction` nulled when censored, an explicit censoring flag and a detection-window end date, with the coherent aggregate retained in diagnostics and the schema bumped.

The project's strongest trait — its refusal to hide uncertainty behind plausible-looking defaults — is visible throughout. The findings above are, with few exceptions, failures to apply that same standard to its own performance evidence, its own gate instruments, and the horizons of its own published grids.
